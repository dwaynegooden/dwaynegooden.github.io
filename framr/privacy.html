FramR privacy policy

Publisher: Kardiac
Effective on the date of FramR's first public release.
Contact: Kardiac, via the app's store listing

FramR adds a frame showing your camera's settings to photos you choose.

Nothing leaves your phone. FramR makes no network calls, has no account and no analytics, and never reads your location. It never sends your photos, your camera information or anything else anywhere.

What FramR collects

Nothing. FramR has no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting. It does not collect, store or transmit personal information.

What FramR does on your device

Reads a photo or video you pick. You choose photos and videos through the system photo picker, or send them to FramR from another app with Share to FramR. Logo files and backups come through the system file picker. FramR never browses your gallery on its own and asks for no storage or media permission.

Reads that photo's EXIF metadata: the camera and lens names, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, exposure compensation and capture date your camera recorded inside the file, so it can draw them into the frame.

Writes a new photo with the frame you chose. On Android, into Pictures/FramR on your own device. On Android, a framed video or moving frame is written as an MP4 into Movies/FramR, or as an animated WebP into Pictures/FramR. Your original is never changed or deleted.

On iPhone, FramR opens photos through the system photo picker and saves exports to Photos with add-only access, so it cannot see the rest of your library.

Keeps your templates, colors, presets and settings, lens nicknames, gear profiles, imported logo files and the last editing session on the device. They are removed when you uninstall the app. If you have Android's Auto Backup turned on for your Google account, it includes your settings only: imported logos are not backed up.

Backup, in Settings, writes a .framr file with your saved styles, lens nicknames, gear profiles and imported logos wherever you choose to save it, and restore reads a .framr file you pick. FramR never sends it anywhere.

The exported copy keeps the original photo's camera information from its EXIF metadata, so that the information stays with the picture. On Android, the camera's serial number is left out by default: where the camera recorded one, the exported copy does not carry it unless you turn on "Keep the camera serial number in exports" in Settings, under Privacy. With that switch on, the exported copy carries the serial number exactly as the original did. On iPhone, the exported copy still carries a recorded serial number, as the original did. Anything you share, you share yourself.

Network

FramR makes no network requests at all. It does not declare the INTERNET permission. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no server for FramR to upload to.

Location

FramR does not read your location. On Android, it does not request ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION. On iPhone, a photo you pick may arrive with its location if you leave Location Included on in the photo picker. Either way, FramR never reads the GPS coordinates a photo may carry in its metadata. The exported copy never contains GPS coordinates: FramR copies only a fixed list of camera tags, and no location tag is on it. A video's location and any telemetry track are never read, and a framed video keeps only the picture and sound tracks, so it carries no location.

Permissions

None. Photo and video access happens entirely through the system picker, which needs no permission, and saving happens through the system media store (on iPhone, add-only Photos access).

Children

FramR is not directed at children under 13. It contains no advertising, no messaging and no user-generated content.

Changes

If this policy changes, the copy inside the app changes in the same release, with a new effective date. If FramR ever gains a feature that uses the network, this policy will say so before that release ships.
